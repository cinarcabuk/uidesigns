Five directions for a film camera that feels machined

Each direction pairs a shooting screen with an editing screen, following your sketch. The look stays black and silver throughout. Every phone is live, so the meters, dials and sliders all respond.

Drag shutter / ISO / ± values Tap the viewfinder to spot-meter Tap the mode dial Fire the shutter Edit, undo, redo

Shared across all five

Exposure modes

Auto, shutter priority, ISO priority and manual. Phones have a fixed aperture (shown as ƒ1.8), so ISO takes the role aperture plays on a real camera. Dragging an auto value switches modes for you: drag the shutter in Auto and you are in S.

The meter

The needle runs on a spring with real overshoot and a small amount of drift as the light changes. In Auto it settles near zero. In Manual it shows how many stops you are off. Spot-metering a dark part of the scene makes it swing.

Editing, four sections

Exp. (exposure, contrast, fade) · Film (stock, strength, warmth) · Texture (grain, halation, light leak) · Print (border, vignette, date stamp). Histogram top right, undo and redo bottom right, as in your sketch.

A

Rangefinder

The closest to your sketch. A full-width 3:4 finder with corner frame lines and a crosshair. The grid toggle sits bottom left and a glass needle meter bottom right. Below that, a calm graphite deck.

  • Shutter, ISO and ± sit in one ruled strip under the finder. Each is a small drum you flick sideways.
  • The star opens the film drawer. The tall rectangle is the last frame, numbered like a contact sheet.
  • The mode dial is a small knob top left. It opens the wheel over the viewfinder.

Strength: the most familiar layout, quick to build. Risk: the least distinctive of the five.

B

Meter Bridge

Built around your favorite reference. A full-width amber needle meter bridges the finder and the deck. Its needle leaves a short phosphor trail, which makes every settings change visible.

  • ISO lives on a rotating gauge. Drag it sideways and the ring turns with a little overshoot.
  • The focal lengths 26 / 35 / 50 mm crop the finder, which suits a film camera better than a zoom slider.
  • The concentric-circle button opens the mode wheel. The glowing dot is the loaded film's color.

Strength: the meter is the brand moment. Risk: the most controls on screen, so it needs careful density.

C

Top Plate

The deck is the silver top plate of an SLR, with engraved labels and knurled drums for shutter and ISO. This is the most literal take on black and silver: a black finder above, brushed aluminum below.

  • A match-needle window sits above a chrome shutter release, as on a 1970s body.
  • The memo holder on the back door shows the loaded film's end tab. Tap it to change rolls.
  • A round exposure counter counts down from 36. The knurled mode dial opens the wheel.

Strength: tactile and ownable, and it matches the film concept best. Risk: skeuomorphism needs restraint so it does not look dated.

D

Pocket

The whole screen is the camera body, in the spirit of Dazz. You look through a chrome-bezeled optical finder. The ten films sit in a carousel under it, so changing film feels like loading a new roll.

  • A round selenium-style light meter sits top right. A silver mode knob sits top left.
  • The settings are printed in amber inside the finder, like an LED readout in a compact camera.
  • Film choice is always one swipe away and never behind a menu.

Strength: the most fun, and the film brands get the spotlight. Risk: a smaller finder, and manual control has to go behind a tap.

E

Hairline

The quietest direction. The image runs full bleed and every control is a one-pixel line or a large light numeral. The mode wheel always peeks from the left edge, as in your last reference, and expands when tapped.

  • A linear match-needle runs the width of the screen, marked in thirds of a stop from −3 to +3.
  • Shutter, ISO and ± are large numerals you drag. Auto values turn grey and say so.
  • The film name runs up the right edge of the image. Tap it to change film.

Strength: the most sleek and premium, and the biggest image. Risk: the analog character relies almost entirely on the needle and the type.
